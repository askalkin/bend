Padel / Tennis Workout Supplements

Bend
Brand
Identity

A kinetic, court-native identity system built from the folder references: electric court blue, tennis-ball acid, chrome supplement packaging, motion blur, precise grid lines, and large condensed type.

Reference Scan

Visual Energy

Most references use movement as the main brand signal: falling tennis balls, blurred athletes, repeated trajectories, spirals, and frozen mid-swing crops.

Color Logic

The repeated base is court blue plus ball yellow, grounded by deep green, black, chrome silver, and white. Clay orange appears as a premium secondary accent.

Type Behavior

The strongest pieces use very large condensed or extended sans typography, often over photography. Editorial serif accents appear occasionally for club/premium attitude.

Product Cue

The existing Bend mockups are strongest when the bottle is treated like sports equipment: metallic, reusable, vertical, and sharply branded rather than like a generic supplement tub.

Primary Mark

BEND
BE ND

Core Bend DNA

Logo Form

Custom heavy uppercase wordmark. The BEND dot behaves like a tennis ball, padel ball, ingredient bead, or product marker.

Split System

Use BE / ND split across paired bottles, two-column web heroes, packaging wraps, and before/after workout states.

Supplement Feel

Clean formula labels, small technical ingredient panels, and chrome/transparent packaging keep the brand credible.

Sport Feel

Court geometry, wall reflections, racket perforations, ball felt, sweat, speed trails, and cropped footwork connect it to padel and tennis.

Rules

Typography

  • H1: condensed or extended black sans, uppercase, 3 to 6 words, line-height 0.82 to 0.92.
  • H2: same family, 42 to 64 px desktop, 34 to 44 px mobile, line-height 0.92 to 1.
  • Body: neutral grotesk, 16 to 18 px, line-height 1.4 to 1.55, max width 68 characters.
  • Micro labels: 10 to 12 px, uppercase, aligned to court grids and product seams.

Logo

  • Default wordmark is BEND in uppercase black or white. Use title case Bend only in body copy.
  • Clearspace equals the width of the ball dot on all sides.
  • Split logo is allowed only when BE and ND can be read together as one brand moment.
  • Do not use gradients inside the logo. Keep the mark flat and brutal.

Graphics

  • Use court lines as layout structure, not decoration: vertical dividers, score cells, trajectories.
  • Pair one hard element with one soft element: chrome with felt, black type with sky, clay with white line.
  • Show product as equipment: metal, clear, reusable, clipped, handled, or strapped to the bag.
  • Avoid bodybuilder supplement tropes. Bend should feel like a court-performance object.
